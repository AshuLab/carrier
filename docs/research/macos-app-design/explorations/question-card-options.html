Question card — directions

What is not working in the screenshot: the card is much lighter and flatter than the other cards (a grey slab where the Notice cards are dark glass), it stacks five kinds of chrome (chip, badge, "Waiting for you", dots, "Choose one") before the question, every option has a filled circle and a ⌥ hint and a two-line body, and Skip floats alone at the bottom. Each direction below changes that. All keep the keyboard model (⌥1…⌥4, ⌘↩ send, ⌥⎋ skip) and show two states: the first question, and the second one (several picks, with what was answered before).

A · the Notice family

Same grammar as the error and sleep cards: badge, the question as the title, one line under it ("Auth method · Choose one"), the actions on the right (progress dots and Skip as text). The options are quiet rows under it, hairlines between them, the ⌥ hint only on hover. Dark glass with a thin amber edge, not a flat fill.

No card at all: an amber bar on the left, a small label, the question, and the options as plain rows with hairlines and a hover fill. It reads as part of the conversation, like a message that asks, and weighs nothing. Skip is text at the bottom.

D · keyboard first

The card chrome of A, but each option is one line: a key cap (⌥1) in place of the circle, the label, and its description inline in grey. The key caps are the interface: the person who types never reaches for the mouse. A selected row turns the key cap green and ends in a check.

What changes in every directionThe card goes to the dark glass of NoticeCard (today's card tints its glass with amber, the Notice cards with black). The number circle no longer carries a fill unless the option is picked. "Waiting for you" goes away: the amber edge already says it. The Back/Send/Skip trio become text buttons that light a pill, as in the queued message. One Other… field per card, styled as in A.
What I'd pickA for the default, with B's chips used automatically when every option's label is short (≤ 16 characters) and has no long description, which is most questions. It keeps the family and gets the weight down where it counts. C is lovely but breaks the pattern of "things that need you are glass cards".
