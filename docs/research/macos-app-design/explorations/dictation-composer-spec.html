Dictation — the microphone in the message box

The entry point of dictation: an icon in the message box, to the left of Send, with no button around it. While it listens there is no word: a visual element shows the sound moving, and that is what tells the person the Mac hears them. The first box is live: click the microphone. Three candidates for that element are side by side below it, then the states and the guide.

Guide to build it

The button lives in MessageBox.swift, which both ConversationView and NewSessionView use, so it is written once. Sizes, weights and structure come from that file.

1. Where it goes, and where it does not

PlaceIn the first cut
Message boxYes, while Dictation is on in Settings. An icon button, mic, in the box's bottom row, left of Send: HStack(spacing: 8) { dictation; button }. With Dictation off the icon is not drawn, not drawn dimmed: the row is the agent select and Send, as it is today.
Settings rail and page headerYes, already drawn: mic on the Dictation page.
Keybindings pageNo. A "Dictate" row goes there when the shortcut arrives.
Menu bar icon, overlay of partial textNo. They belong to the global dictation.
macOS's own orange microphone dotAppears by itself while the Mac listens; nothing to build.

2. The button

PartValue
ShapeNo glass and no border: .buttonStyle(.plain) over a 36 × 36 frame, the same as Send's, with contentShape(Circle()). Send is the only button of the box with a drawn shape, which makes it the screen's one action.
Hover and pressA circle of the same size and shape as Send's: secondary at 14% under the pointer, 20% pressed; the icon goes from secondary to primary. The two controls share one outline, so the row reads as a pair. A button with no border needs this, or it does not look pressable.
IconImage(systemName: "mic") at .scaled(16, weight: .regular), the size it had inside the circle. Blocked: mic.slash.
AlignmentIts center on Send's center, in a frame of the same 36 points, so the two circles line up in the HStack.
ColorIdle: secondary, primary on hover. Listening: Color.themeAccent. Never red: red is for errors here, and a person who dictates is not in one.
WordsNone while it listens. The two states where a spinner cannot say what is awaited keep a short caption in the bottom row, .scaled(12) secondary, right aligned: "Getting the model ready…" and "Transcribing…". The box can be as narrow as 320 points, so the caption sits in ViewThatFits and goes first when the row is tight.
Tooltip"Dictate" idle, "Stop dictation" while listening; the shortcut goes in it when there is one, as Send's does.

3. What says "listening" without a word

What it isForAgainst
A · icon onlyThe microphone in the accent, its capsule filling from the bottom with the level, like the Mac's own Dictation.The smallest change; costs no width.On its own it is the quietest: a person who does not know the convention may not read "listening", and in a quiet room it hardly moves.
B · waveform across the rowThe free space between the agent select and the icon becomes a waveform of the last few seconds of level, scrolling to the left; the microphone turns solid in the accent.Self-explanatory: a moving waveform next to a microphone is understood without a legend. It takes the width the row has, so it fits any window.The row changes more than in the others. In a narrow box it has little room (it shows as many samples as fit, and is left out under 24 points).
C · accent edgeThe box's outline turns to the accent and breathes with the level, with the microphone filling as in A.Readable from across the room; the whole box says "this is live".It is the only one that touches the box, and it is a mode on the box itself, heavier than the others; a person typing while it is on sees an accent border around their text.
  • Chosen: C. The whole box says it is live, readable from across the room, and the row keeps its layout: nothing is added to it and nothing has to fit in a narrow window. A (the icon alone) was too quiet, and B (a waveform across the row) changed the row and needed a rule for narrow boxes. They are kept on this page as the record.
  • Source. The level comes from the audio capture of the core, which is shared by every recognizer, so it is computed once: the root mean square of each buffer, in decibels, with a noise floor (about −50 dB is zero) and a ceiling (about −10 dB is full), smoothed so it falls slower than it rises. Published about 15 times a second; the waveform keeps the last 64 samples, one bar each, so about four seconds. It is a pure function of the samples, the one piece of this feature that has a plain unit test.
  • Drawing, in three layers over the box's glass and under its text, all with allowsHitTesting(false) and the box's own radius (16):
    • The edge. RoundedRectangle(cornerRadius: 16).strokeBorder(AngularGradient(colors: [c1, c2, c3, c1], center: .center, angle: phase), lineWidth: 1.5). The angle turns slowly (about 50° a second) so the colors travel round the box. Its opacity follows the level, 40% in silence to 100% at full voice, so it is seen when nobody talks and breathes when they do.
    • The halo. The same stroke, 7 points wide and blurred 8, at half the edge's opacity, clipped to the box: a glow just inside the line.
    • The light. A MeshGradient (3 × 3 points; macOS 26 has it) whose points drift with the clock and whose brightness follows the level, masked so it is strongest toward the edges and keeps a quarter of its strength in the middle, where the text is. Its opacity tops at about 60% at full voice and moves with the level (35% of that in silence), so under the text it is about 15%.
    The microphone fills with the level as in A, so the edge, the light and the icon agree. Everything comes and goes with .animation(.smooth(duration: 0.2)), as the box's other changes do.
  • Colors. Three, derived from Color.themeAccent: the accent itself and its hue turned 40° either way, same saturation and brightness. A fixed palette is out: the accent is the person's. With a gray or nearly gray accent (saturation under 15%) the hues do not turn; only brightness varies.
  • Cost. A TimelineView(.animation(minimumInterval: 1.0 / 30)) that exists only while listening, with the blurred layers in a drawingGroup(); when listening stops the view is gone and nothing runs. To be measured on a battery in the real app.
  • Reduce Motion. The angle stops turning and the light stops drifting; the level still sets the brightness, because that is information. Reduce Transparency: the halo and the light go and the edge stays.
  • Legibility. Under the text the light is about 15% at full voice, and the partial text is secondary: on a bright accent the contrast has to be checked in the real app, light and dark. If it is not enough, the middle of the mask goes down before the edges do.
  • The file drop. A file dragged over the box already draws a veil and a dashed accent border. If both happen, the drop wins and the edge waits: the dashed border is the one that asks for an action.
  • Motion. The indicator is information, not decoration: it follows the voice also with Reduce Motion on. There is no ring or pulse, so nothing to switch off.

  • Getting ready covers the first time a model loads onto the Neural Engine and the time Apple takes to fetch the assets of a language: both take seconds and the person should see why nothing happens.
  • Failed to start is a red line under the box, .scaled(12), like model.refusal, with the cause the core knows (no input device, another app holds the microphone).

5. Behaviour (proposed; the spec confirms)

  • Start and stop. One click starts, the next stops: for now that is the only way to stop it. Escape and the other keys come with the shortcuts, later.
  • Where the text goes. At the caret, as a range the field knows is tentative: drawn in secondary and replaced as the model revises it, made normal on the final. Text already in the box is not touched.
  • Typing while listening. A key the person types ends the listening and keeps everything so far, so the box never has two writers.
  • Enter while listening. It ends the listening and does not send; Send is disabled until it stops, and a second Enter sends. A half sentence is never sent, and a model that gives its text at the end has no text yet to send.
  • Leaving. Switching session, closing the window or putting the app in the background stops the listening and keeps what was heard. It is not a gesture to stop but a rule so that nothing keeps recording unseen.
  • Turning Dictation off in Settings while listening stops it the same way.
  • Privacy. The audio stays in memory and is dropped when it stops, as the brief says.

6. Accessibility

  • Nothing on screen says "listening" in words, so the announcement has to: "Listening" when it starts, "Dictation stopped, 12 words added" when it ends, and the cause on a failure. Partial text is not announced, because it would speak over the person.
  • Labels: "Dictate", "Stop dictation", "Dictation is getting ready", "Transcribing", "Microphone is not allowed". The waveform and the edge are hidden from VoiceOver.
  • Reachable by keyboard in the box's order, before Send, with a focus ring as the other buttons have. The popover of a blocked microphone takes focus and Escape closes it.
  • State is never color alone: the icon changes (mic, filled, mic.slash, a spinner) and, in B, the waveform moves in a place nothing else moves.

7. Open

  • How much light. Level 3, the loudest, was chosen. It is the one that can tire in a long dictation; level 2 (the same layers with the light at about half) is the fallback, and it is a single number to lower. The hue turn (40°) and the opacities are first numbers too.
  • The edge's range. 40% to 100% and 1.5 points are the first numbers. In light themes and with a very pale accent the 40% may be too faint; to be tuned in the real app, with the accents the person can pick.
  • Icon size. Kept at the 16 points it had inside the circle, as asked. Without the circle it reads smaller beside Send's 36, and at 16 the level fill of A is small. 18 would balance it and costs nothing in the row, since the hit and hover circle stay at 36. To be tried in the real app, at true pixels.
  • The two spinner states keep their words. A spinner does not say what is awaited, so "Getting the model ready…" and "Transcribing…" stay. If they should be wordless too, the tooltip would have to carry them.
  • Escape is not part of the first cut. When it arrives with the shortcuts, the box already uses it for the command list; the list should win, as it is the nearer.
