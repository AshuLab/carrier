# Dictation

Dictation in the message boxes and in any app: the recognizers, the microphone, the Dictation page of [Settings](settings.md), the speech models and the [global dictation](#global-dictation).

## Recognizer and microphone

A real build registers Apple's recognizer (`Carrier/AppleRecognizer.swift`, over SpeechAnalyzer: native partial text, nothing to download, the language chosen on the Dictation page, which starts on the first language of macOS that it supports) and the microphone capture (`Carrier/MicrophoneCapture.swift`, which gives the buffers to the recognizer and the level, `CarrierCore/AudioLevel.swift`, about thirty times a second).
The first click asks macOS for the microphone; a denied one draws `mic.slash` and a click opens a popover with Open System Settings; a failure to start shows its cause in red under the box and is appended, cause only, to `<Carrier folder>/app/logs/dictation.log` (`DictationLog.swift`).
SpeechAnalyzer needs only the microphone permission, not `NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription`.

## The model ready before the press

Keep the speech model ready (`dictation.model.keepReady`, on by default) loads the chosen model in the background when Carrier opens with Dictation on (a moment after its window is drawn), when Dictation is turned on, when Keep it ready is turned on and when the chosen model or language changes, so the first dictation does not say "Getting the model ready…" for long.
Release the model after (`dictation.model.releaseAfter`: 5, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, or Never, 15 by default; a saved value that is not on offer reads as 15) frees the model of someone who is not dictating, whatever the switch says: the minutes count from the end of the last dictation (from the load, when nobody has dictated yet), the next dictation loads the model again and says "Getting the model ready…", and Never keeps it until Carrier quits.
With the switch off a model loads at the first dictation, as before, and the release still applies; with Dictation off nothing loads, and turning Dictation off releases what was loaded.
Both rows are drawn at 40% with Dictation off (they are in Memory, on the Speech recognition screen), and `reset(.dictation)` puts them back.
Choosing another model lets go of the model of another recognizer before the new one loads.
Apple's model is kept by the system while it is ready (the analyzer is created with `modelRetention: .processLifetime`) and the retention ends when it is released (`SpeechModels.endRetention()`).
Apple's transcriber is the progressive preset without `fastResults`, whose documentation says it trades accuracy for speed; the volatile results and every other option are as the preset has them.
A background load that fails is only appended to `dictation.log`: the person is told at the dictation, which tries again.
Memory, measured on the Mac it was built on (the process's physical footprint, which does not count what Core ML keeps for the Neural Engine and the GPU, so the real figure is higher): Nemotron 3.5 Multilingual, the one speech model downloaded there, added about 50 MB once loaded and about 60 MB after a pass over audio; Whisper and the other FluidAudio models were not downloaded and are not measured.

Each dictation appends one line to `dictation.log` with where its time went and no text of the dictation: `timing capture=40 first-audio=45 model-ready=2300 first-word=3100 final=180`, in milliseconds from the press to the capture running, to the first audio, to the model ready and to the first word shown, and from the stop to the final text (a moment that did not happen is left out, and a dictation dropped before the capture started writes none).

## The microphone starts at the press

The capture starts at the press, whether or not the model is ready (`DictationSession.run`), and the audio is kept until the recognizer is ready and then given to it from the first sample, in order, so the words said while the model gets ready end up in the text.
The first dictation ever still asks macOS for the microphone before anything is captured, and a denied microphone behaves as before: both are the controller's, ahead of the session.
From the press the message box and the widget draw what they draw when they listen (the line, the filled microphone and the clock) with "Getting the model ready…" where the caption goes (`dictationPresentation(_:registered:microphone:modelReady:)`), until the model is ready; then the words catch up.
The start sound plays when the first audio arrives, not when the capture starts.
A stop while the model gets ready (a click, the shortcut, a release in Hold, Enter) ends the capture and keeps what was captured: the box draws the spinner until the model is ready, which then reads it, and the dictation ends as a stop while listening does.
A stop with no audio captured yet, as a release right after the press in Hold, pastes nothing.
Esc stops the capture and writes nothing.
A load or a release the controller queued before the press ends before the recognizer prepares for the dictation (`DictationSession.beforePrepare`), so none of them can drop what it loaded.
A model that is not ready 30 s after the press (`DictationSession.answerFloor`) stops the capture, drops what was heard and fails as a dictation that could not start, with "The speech model did not get ready in time." shown and logged.

## Firm and tentative text

A partial event carries two texts, `DictationEvent.partial(firm:tentative:)`: the firm words will not change while the person speaks, and the tentative ones follow them and may.
The message box and the widget draw the firm words as normal text and only the tentative ones as partial text (the normal colour with a touch of the accent and a dotted underline), so the text no longer reads as a guess from its first word to its last.
The widget's text still scrolls with the newest words on the last line.
Each recognizer fills both by its own rule:

- The core's chunking (Parakeet TDT v3, `ChunkPolicy.firmText` and `tentativeText`): the words it has settled are firm and the words of the last pass over the open segment are tentative.
- Apple (`AppleRecognizer.partial(settled:volatile:)`): the final results are firm and the volatile ones tentative.
- The streaming models (Nemotron, Parakeet EOU and Parakeet Unified, `DictationEvent.growing`): their text only grows, so every word but the last is firm and the last, which may still grow by a piece of a word, is tentative.
- The fixture recognizer: each word is firm as the next is given, and the last one is tentative.
- A model that gives its text at the end has no partial text.

The space that sets the firm words apart from the tentative ones belongs to the firm part, so the dotted line starts at the first tentative word.
`TentativeText` keeps one range for the dictation's text, which the next partial replaces whole, and a tentative tail inside it (`tentativeRange`), the only part the field is told to draw as partial.
An edit by the person during a dictation, a cleanup and a cancel behave as before, over the whole range, and a stop keeps all the text, firm and tentative, as final.

## "Transcribing…" only when it is slow

After a stop, a model that gives its text at the end shows the spinner at once and says "Transcribing…" only when the final text has not come 250 ms after the stop (`DictationSession.quickAnswer`, `answerIsSlow`, on the injected clock); a final text within 250 ms never shows it.
The 30 s the recognizer has to answer is its own clock.

## Under `--fixture`

Under `--fixture` the app registers the fixture recognizer instead (`CarrierCore/FixtureRecognizer.swift`) and asks macOS for nothing: the microphone in the message box of the new session and of a conversation answers a click with a canned Spanish phrase, word by word as partial text (the words before the last as normal text, the last one in the normal colour with a touch of the accent and a dotted underline) and then final (plain), at the caret.
`--fixture-dictation-at-the-end` gives the phrase only after the click that stops it (the box says "Transcribing…" if it takes more than 250 ms) and `--fixture-dictation-fails` fails to start with a cause shown in red under the box; the global shortcut dictates the same canned phrase into the widget.

## The Dictation page

The Dictation page is a native Form like the Providers page: a first screen and six screens it opens, each a place of the window's history (‹ ›) with its name and a line of what it holds under the title (`DictationScreen` in `CarrierCore/DictationReadiness.swift`, drawn by `Carrier/DictationTab.swift`, each group its own view; `SettingsPage.dictation`, in the App group after Keybindings, because it is global and not of the chat).
The first screen has the status card (`DictationStatusCard`), then Set up (Speech recognition, Global dictation, Cleanup and Sounds) and Your dictations (History and Statistics), each row with what its screen says now at its right ("Parakeet TDT v3 · English", "⌥⇧Space · Toggle · Medium" or Off, "On · LM Studio" or Off, "Tink · Pop · 60%", "48 dictations", "35 in 30 days").
The status card has the switch (`dictation.enabled`, on by default; off takes the microphone out of both boxes at once, asks macOS for nothing and stops a dictation that listens, keeping its text) and says whether dictation works now, as `dictationReadiness` decides: "Dictation is off"; "Cannot listen yet" when macOS has the microphone blocked; "Ready to dictate"; or "Ready, with one thing to fix" (then "with N things to fix") when Accessibility is not allowed (the text goes on the clipboard instead of being pasted; only while Dictate in any app is on) or the chosen speech model is not on disk (another listens meanwhile; a download in progress is not a thing to fix).
It lists the three checks (the microphone's permission, Accessibility from `AXIsProcessTrusted()`, read again each time Carrier comes to the front, and the model in use) and, for what macOS has to be told, a button that opens its pane of System Settings (Open Microphone Settings, Open Accessibility Settings).
Reset, in the toolbar, is the first screen's alone, and asks first: it puts the whole page back (`reset(.dictation)`).
The screens:

- Speech recognition: the speech models table (`Carrier/SpeechModelsTable.swift`; the rows and what each offers are `speechModelRows` in `CarrierCore/SpeechModelsTable.swift`: a recognizer filter, a radio on the downloaded rows that chooses the model, Download with its size, a progress bar with Cancel, the cause with Retry, a trash, and the licence linking to the model's card; a line over it when the chosen model is not on disk), the Language popup (`dictation.language`; `languageChoices` in `CarrierCore/DictationSettings.swift` says what each model offers; an unknown model reads as Apple's), Memory (Keep the speech model ready and Release the model after, [below](#the-model-ready-before-the-press)) and Storage (the size of the files under `<Carrier folder>/app/cache/models`, with Show in Finder).
- Global dictation: Dictate in any app (`dictation.global`, on by default, which turns the shortcut off with it when off), the Accessibility row, the Shortcut and the Activation, and under them the Widget group: the style as four drawn cards, the position and a preview ([below](#global-dictation)).
- Cleanup and Sounds: [below](#cleanup) and [below](#sounds).
- [History](#history) and [Statistics](#statistics).

With the switch off the settings inside Speech recognition, Global dictation, Cleanup and Sounds are drawn at 40% and take no clicks (`dictationRowIsLive`; Storage stays live); the rows that open a screen, History and Statistics stay as they are.

## Global dictation

The shortcut (⌥⇧ Space until the person records another) dictates in any app (`Carrier/GlobalHotKey.swift`, a system hot key, which needs no permission): it opens a floating widget over the display of the window in front, in the style and place the person chose ([below](#the-widget-style-and-position)), that never takes the focus from the app the person is in, and that can be dragged (`Carrier/DictationWidget.swift`).
The widget shows the text as it is said (unless the person turned Live text off, [below](#the-widget-style-and-position)), with the firm and tentative words drawn as in the message box ([firm and tentative text](#firm-and-tentative-text)), the time and the keys (`⌥⇧ Space to finish · Esc to cancel`), with the same wait captions as the message box (Getting the model ready…, and Transcribing… when the final text is slow).
By default it is a toggle: the shortcut starts the dictation, the next press ends it and pastes the text once at the cursor of the app in front, and the widget closes.
In Hold the dictation listens while the keys are down and the release ends it and pastes, as the second press does (a hot key gives the key release, `GlobalHotKey`'s `released`); the widget says "Release ⌥⇧ Space to finish".
A release right after the press pastes nothing and closes with no trace, and a release while the model is getting ready ends the capture and pastes what it captured once the model is ready ([the microphone starts at the press](#the-microphone-starts-at-the-press)); Esc while the keys are held cancels, and the release after it does nothing.
The mode is read when the dictation starts.
With Carrier in front on a session, the release ends the message box's dictation too, whether it is listening, loading the model or waiting for the microphone prompt.
Esc, which Carrier holds only while the widget is open, cancels in any state: nothing is pasted and the clipboard is as it was.
A dictation with no words pastes nothing and closes with no trace.
A failure to start (a blocked microphone, no speech model, the capture) or to recognize shows its cause in red in the widget, with no clock and the words "Esc to dismiss", and it stays until Esc or the shortcut, and a line with the cause only is appended to `dictation.log`.
The first dictation asks macOS for the microphone.

The paste (`Carrier/ClipboardPaste.swift`) saves every item of the clipboard with every type, writes the text marked as transient (clipboard managers skip it) and for this Mac only, sends a ⌘V after the keys of the shortcut are up, waits 200 ms and puts the clipboard back as it was; if the person copied something meanwhile their copy stays.
When the dictation ends, `pasteDecision` (`CarrierCore/PasteDecision.swift`) decides from what the system says about the permission and the field with the cursor (`Carrier/FocusedField.swift`: the focused element of the app in front, its role and subrole, never its value): an editable field with Accessibility on is pasted; a secure field (the subrole says so, or macOS has secure input on), no field at all and an unreadable focus are only copied, and without Accessibility it is never a paste.
A text that was only copied stays in the widget, which becomes a box (the Small one when the style is the Pill) and takes clicks, with the text complete and scrollable (its scroll bar showing) and the reason in orange over it, not inside what scrolls (No text field has the cursor; The field is secure; Accessibility is off for Carrier, then "It is on the clipboard"; the box has three more lines than its style for it).
Its buttons are Copy (puts the text on the clipboard again and says Copied) and Dismiss, and Open System Settings (opens Privacy & Security › Accessibility) when the reason is the permission, and Reset permission beside it. Carrier is signed ad hoc, so macOS ties Accessibility to the exact build: after an update the switch still reads on while `AXIsProcessTrusted()` is false. Reset permission (`FocusedField.resetPermission`, also on the Accessibility row of Settings while it is not allowed) runs `tccutil reset Accessibility <bundle id>` and asks again, and the person turns the switch on once more. A signing identity that stays the same across builds would make it unnecessary.
It stays until Dismiss, Esc, or the shortcut, which closes it and starts a new dictation.
This is the one state in which the panel is the key window (still without activating Carrier), so the keyboard reaches it: Tab moves between the buttons with Full Keyboard Access, Return is Copy and Esc is Dismiss, handled by the panel itself because Carrier holds no Esc hot key while a text is left open.
The buttons keep only their symbols when the box is too narrow for their words.
VoiceOver is told the reason, a failure's cause and a paste without cleaning when they appear, since the panel is not in front of it.
The first time the person turns on Dictate in any app (`dictation.accessibilityExplained`, which Reset leaves alone) with Accessibility not allowed, the page explains what it is for (to see which field has the cursor and press ⌘V; it never reads the fields) and then asks macOS; if the person says Not now it asks again next time, and it never explains once it has.
The terminals and the web inputs of apps that build their accessibility tree only for assistive technology may read as no field, so a dictation there is copied: whether to paste anyway is for the owner to decide after trying them.
`GlobalDictationController` (`Carrier/GlobalDictationController.swift`) joins the machine of `CarrierCore/GlobalDictation.swift` to the hot key, the widget and the paste, and registers the shortcut only while the Dictation page's Dictate in any app and Dictation switches are both on.
The widget runs on the one `DictationSession` of the message boxes, so a dictation in a box and one in the widget never run together: the shortcut stops the box's.
Hiding, minimizing or closing Carrier's window, or going to another app, stops a message box's dictation, which nothing would show, and never the widget's, which stays over the app the person is in (`DictationController.stopBoxDictation`).
A box's dictation that waits for the widget's to end is dropped, so none opens the microphone unseen, and one that is cleaning finishes its cleanup, the microphone being off already.
The Global dictation group has two more rows (`dictation.shortcut` and `dictation.activation`, both put back by `reset(.dictation)`): Shortcut (the keys shown, Record to press new ones, Esc or Cancel to keep the old, a refusal as the row's own orange problem and VoiceOver told of it and of the keys accepted; an accepted combination replaces the registered one at once and is kept after a restart; it starts at ⌥⇧ Space, not ⌥ Space, which ChatGPT and Handy hold, and the spec's ⌥ Space reads as ⌥⇧ Space) and Activation (Toggle or Hold, Toggle by default).
The recorder (`recordShortcut` in `CarrierCore/DictationShortcut.swift`) refuses a combination with none of ⌃, ⌥ or ⌘ (⇧ alone would stop the key from typing) with a line that says why and goes on listening, and a key that has no name and no printable character; while it listens the shortcut is let go, so pressing it records it instead of dictating. A saved shortcut that does not pass the same rules reads as the default. A shortcut that another app holds is not told by the system.
With Carrier in front on a session, or on the draft of a new one (before the first message is sent, whether or not a workspace is chosen), the shortcut does not open the widget: it focuses that message box and dictates into it as the microphone button does (the partial text and the listening line), and the shortcut again ends it, with the text kept in the box. In Settings, an epic or any other view of Carrier, and in any other app, it opens the widget, which pastes into Carrier's focused field if there is one.
The rule is `dictationFront` and `dictationRoute(for:)` in `CarrierCore/DictationRoute.swift` (`.session` and `.draft` go to the box): Carrier's main window (or a sheet over it) or its Settings window must be the key window, the Settings window counts as Settings, and an archived session, which has no message box, uses the widget. A dictation that the widget already runs is ended by the shortcut wherever the person is. With the Dictation switch off, or Dictate in any app, the shortcut is not registered, so nothing happens in Carrier either.

### The widget style and position

The Widget group of the Global dictation screen has a Widget style row (four drawn cards: Pill, Small, Medium, Large; Medium by default), a Widget theme row (five drawn swatches over a bright backdrop: Default, Black, Dark glass, Clear glass (the system's glass, untinted), Theme glow; Default is the accent's glass as it always was), a Live text switch (on by default), a Position row (Top, Bottom; Bottom by default, and Custom while there is a dragged place) and under them a Preview of the chosen look, listening to a sample, that changes as the rows do and shrinks to the width of the card when the box is wider than it (Large in a narrow window) (`dictation.widget.style`, `.theme`, `.preview`, `.position` and `.spot`, all put back by `reset(.dictation)`; a saved value that is not one of them reads as the default). The widget reads them each time it opens.
With Live text off the widget is the same box (the style's width and text size, the same surface) with no lines of text: only the row with the time and the keys (`DictationWidgetBox.showsText`, a box of 0 lines, 52 points high), which says "Getting the model ready…" or "Transcribing…" in the place of the keys while it waits. The Pill is the same with no text to hide. A failure and a text that was only copied still show their text, since they need it.
The widget can be dragged: a drag that starts on the box moves it, and where it is let go is kept (`dictation.widget.spot`, `DictationWidgetSpot`: the centre as a share of the display's width, the nearer edge, top or bottom, and the gap in points from it). The next dictation opens there, on the display of the window in front, scaled to it and never outside what the menu bar and the Dock leave. Because it hangs from its nearer edge, the Pill, a box and a box that grows for a copied text open away from that edge instead of jumping. The widget takes the pointer for this, so it no longer lets clicks through where it is; to get it out of the way, drag it. Choosing Top or Bottom in the Position row forgets the spot. In the state that holds a copied text the text is the person's to select, so only the margins of the box drag it.
Small, Medium and Large are a box of 2, 3 and 4 lines, 340, 420 and 540 points wide, with 12, 14 and 17 point text (at the person's text size), and the box has one height per style; the text starts at the top, and when it is longer than the lines it scrolls, the newest words last. The Pill is a capsule badge with no text: the microphone in the accent while it listens, a spinner while it gets ready or transcribes, a sparkle while it cleans, a check when pasted, a warning when it pasted without cleaning and an x when cancelled.
In a box, a pasted or cancelled dictation shows its text with Pasted or Cancelled under it, and one pasted without cleaning shows Pasted without cleaning and the cause in orange (the Pill says it to VoiceOver). A check or an x stays for 1.2 seconds (the warning, 4, since it holds the cause) and fades (with no animation under Reduce Motion); a new dictation takes it away at once. A dictation with no words, a failure and a text that was only copied show no check or x.
The states that need text or a button (a failure to start or to recognize, and a text that was only copied) open the Pill as the Small box until they are dismissed; with another style they use that box.
The panel's size is `DictationWidget.place()`'s alone (the hosting view has no sizing options: with its own, a state with a scrolling text and buttons made the panel as tall as the display): the look's size, plus, for a text that was only copied, the height the notices need beyond the three lines the box allows (`dictationKeptNotices`, measured at the box's text size and width, since a reason wraps in a narrow box), and never more than the display leaves around it.
Top puts the widget at the top centre of the display under the menu bar and Bottom at the bottom centre above the Dock, 24 points from the edge of what the menu bar and the Dock leave (`dictationWidgetOrigin`).
What the widget draws for a state and a style is `dictationWidgetLook` in `CarrierCore/DictationWidgetLook.swift`, from what `GlobalDictation.shown` says.

The widget is glass in the app's accent (`WidgetSurface` in `Carrier/DictationWidget.swift`, over the chat's `chatSurface`, so Settings › Chat › Surfaces turns it flat too) and, only while it listens, it draws the message box's own line, `DictationIndicator`, outside its edge with the radius of the box (the capsule's for the Pill), driven by the voice; Reduce Motion and Reduce Transparency do what they do in the message box.
The panel is 14 points larger than the box on every side to hold the line and the shadow; that clear margin takes the pointer too, so it covers 14 points of the app beneath.
While it listens the box holds only the text, the time, the shortcut and Esc.
The text starts at the top and goes down; when it is longer than the lines of the style it scrolls so the newest words are on the last line, and the first line fades out (`WidgetBox`, which reads the text's height and asks `dictationTextFit` for the lines that are visible and whether the top fades). The text that was only copied is the person's to scroll, from the top, with no fade.

## History

Every dictation that ends in the widget or in a message box is written to a history, which the History screen of the Dictation page lists (`Carrier/HistoryGroup.swift`, over `CarrierCore/DictationRecords.swift` and [0031](../adr/0031-dictation-keeps-a-history-and-its-statistics-live-apart-from-it.md)).
It starts with Keep the text of my dictations (`dictation.history.keepText`, on by default, put back by `reset(.dictation)`), which says that the text is kept in plain text on this Mac: off, nothing new is recorded and the entries that exist stay.
The store is `<Carrier folder>/app/state/dictations.jsonl`, one JSON entry per line (readable by the account only), read into memory, off the main thread, a moment after the window is drawn (or when the History screen opens), and filtered there; `dictation.log` still never holds text.
An entry has the time it started, the text as recognized, the text after cleanup (empty when Cleanup did not run on it or failed), the speech model, the seconds the recognizer took after the person stopped (for a model that gives its text at the end, the transcription itself), the cleanup model and its time, the seconds of audio, the words, where it went and how it ended.
It went to the name of the app a paste reached, to Carrier when it was dictated in a message box, or to the widget only when it was copied, cancelled or failed; it ended pasted (a box counts the text left in it as pasted), copied, cancelled (the text heard until Esc) or failed (the text heard until the failure).
A dictation with no words records nothing, and neither does one that could not start.
The widget's entries are written by `GlobalDictation`, which knows how it ended, and the message boxes' by `DictationController` from the session's `DictationTake`, what the dictation heard; both go through `DictationRecords.record`, which is where the switch and the words are checked.
Under `--fixture` and in the tests the history is in a temporary folder, never in the Carrier folder.

The list is grouped by day, newest first (Today, Yesterday, then the weekday and the date), under a search over the text, the cleaned text and the destination, and the filters All, Pasted, Copied, Cancelled and Failed (`dictationEntries`, `dictationDays`).
It draws the first 100 entries that match (`dictationHistoryPage`) and, under them, a Show 100 more button; the line at the bottom says how many match of how many there are.
A new search or filter goes back to the first 100; a new or a deleted dictation leaves the list at the page it was.
The list is worked out when one of those changes and kept, not at each redraw (`dictationHistory`).
A row shows the time, the text (two lines) and chips with the outcome, the destination, the model, the transcription time, the words and the length; a click opens its detail (the text as recognized and as cleaned, the model, the transcription time for the audio length, the cleanup and where it went).
Copy puts the text on the clipboard (the cleaned text when there is one) and says Copied for a moment, and the trash asks Delete this dictation? first (its text and its audio go, the statistics stay), with the switch on or off; an empty list says No dictations yet, and a search or filter with no match says Nothing matches.

### Audio

Keep the audio (`dictation.history.keepAudio`, off by default) saves the audio of each dictation that is recorded as `<Carrier folder>/app/state/audio/<entry id>.wav`, a 16-bit mono WAV readable by the account only; with it off no audio is written, and a dictation that is not recorded (no words, or Keep the text off) has none.
The session gathers the samples only if the switch is on as the dictation starts and gives them in the `DictationTake`; the entry does not point to the file, which is named for its id, so `DictationRecords` knows what has audio by listing the folder (`audioIDs`, `audioURL(of:)`).
Delete audio older than (`dictation.history.deleteAudioAfter`: 7 days, 30 days, 90 days or Never, 30 by default) deletes the audio of the entries that started before the limit a moment after the window is drawn, once a day while it runs (`DictationController.startAudioSweep`) and at once when the choice changes; the entries stay.
Audio on disk says how much there is ("182 MB in 214 files"), and Delete all audio, after a confirmation, deletes every audio file and keeps every transcript. Deleting an entry deletes its audio.
An entry with audio shows a player in its detail (`Carrier/AudioPlayerView.swift`, over `AVAudioPlayer`): Play and Pause, the place it has got to and the time; it stops when the row closes.
`reset(.dictation)` puts the two switches back and never deletes audio.

## Statistics

The Statistics screen of the Dictation page (`Carrier/StatisticsGroup.swift`) shows how much the person dictates, from counters kept apart from the history ([0031](../adr/0031-dictation-keeps-a-history-and-its-statistics-live-apart-from-it.md)).
The counters are `<Carrier folder>/app/state/dictation-statistics.json`: per day, hour and speech model, the dictations, the words, the seconds dictated and the longest one, and no text.
`DictationRecords.record` counts a dictation that ended pasted or copied with words, whatever Keep the text says; a cancelled, failed or empty one is not counted.
Deleting entries and turning the history off leave the counters as they were, and Reset statistics is the only thing that deletes them.
A day is the person's calendar day (`dictationDayKey`), so a change of time zone does not move what was counted.
Under `--fixture` and in the tests the counters are in a temporary folder.

The screen starts with Overview, with a range, 7 days, 30 days (the one it opens on), 90 days or All time, over four cards: Dictations, Words, Speaking time and Time saved, each with the change against the period just before it ("▲ 12% vs previous", nothing for All time, which has no period before it, or when the period before had none).
Under them is a chart of words or dictations by day, with the busiest day in the full accent.
Then, one under the other, By hour of the day and This week.
By hour of the day is a strip of 24 cells, one per hour, as strong as the dictations in that hour of the days of the chosen range, with a line that says the hour the person dictates most ("You dictate most around 15:00."; the earliest on a tie; "Nothing dictated in this range yet." with none).
This week always compares the last 7 days (today included) with the 7 before, whatever the range: a sentence with the words, the dictations, the change in percent of the words, the busiest day and the time saved, and a small chart of words by day with the busiest in the full accent.
It says what it can: a previous week with no words says there is nothing to compare with instead of a percent, a week with no dictations says so (and what the week before had), and a time saved of zero is left out.
Records, over all the counters and not the range, has the most dictations in a day, the most words in a day, the longest dictation and the longest streak of days in a row with a dictation; on a tie a record keeps the earliest day, which is when it was reached, and a day with no dictation ends a streak.
With no counters (nothing dictated yet, or after Reset) it says No statistics yet and what it needs: a dictation that ends with the text pasted or copied.
The figures are pure functions of the counters (`dictationFigures`, `dictationHours`, `weeklySummary`, `statisticsRecords`, `statisticsChange` and `statisticsDuration` in `CarrierCore/DictationStatistics.swift`, tested in `DictationStatisticsTests`).
The time saved is `timeSaved(words:speaking:wpm:)` as the [spec](../specs/global-dictation.md) writes it: the words over the typing speed, minus the time spent speaking, never negative.

At the bottom, Statistics settings has the Typing speed (`dictation.statistics.wpm`, 40 words a minute by default, from 10 to 200, put back by `reset(.dictation)`) and Reset statistics, which asks first, deletes the counters and leaves the history as it is.

## Sounds

The Sounds group (`Carrier/SoundsGroup.swift`) has a Start sound and an End sound, each with a play button, a popup of three of macOS's own sounds (Tink, Pop and Glass; Tink starts and Pop ends) and a switch, and one Volume from 0 to 100 that starts at 60 and serves both (`dictation.sound.start.on`, `dictation.sound.start`, `dictation.sound.end.on`, `dictation.sound.end` and `dictation.sound.volume`, all put back by `reset(.dictation)`; a saved sound that is not on offer reads as the default).
The sounds belong to the global dictation: the message boxes stay silent.
The start sound plays when the first audio of the widget's dictation arrives; the end sound plays when the shortcut stops listening and goes on to clean and paste, whether or not words were heard; Esc plays no end sound, since nothing goes on, and a dictation that never listened (a blocked microphone, a failure to start) plays none.
A switch off silences its sound and volume 0 silences both, but the play button always plays, at the volume set.
Which sound a moment plays is a pure function, `dictationSound(for:_:)` in `CarrierCore/DictationSounds.swift`, fed by the moments the machine reports (`GlobalDictationMoment`: `listening`, `stopped`, `cancelled`); `Carrier/DictationSoundPlayer.swift` plays it through `NSSound`.

## Speech models

`ModelStore` (`CarrierCore/ModelStore.swift`) owns the states of the models behind a downloader port: a model lives in `app/cache/models/<recognizer>/<model>/` and counts as downloaded when a `complete` file is there, written last, so a folder without it, left by a cancel, a failure or an app that quit, is deleted (at once, or at the next launch); a failure says no connection, no space on the disk or a file that did not check; the model in use and Apple's, which the system manages, have no delete; and nothing outside the listed models can be downloaded (`SpeechModelCatalog.problems` is the rules of the list: a unique id, a licence, a size or Apple's system marker, an https card on an allowed host).
A real build lists Apple's model, four of Whisper's (below) and six of FluidAudio's (`Carrier/FluidAudioRecognizer.swift`, on Core ML, each with the licence and the card of its model): Parakeet TDT v3 and Parakeet Ultra (25 languages with detection of the language, CC-BY-4.0; both are batch models that the core's chunking runs, `CarrierCore/ChunkedPartials.swift`, tested in `ChunkedPartialsTests`: TDT v3 shows partial text, since one pass over 10 s of audio runs at 0.015 of real time, under the rule's 0.05, by transcribing the audio of the open segment again every second of audio, a pause of 150 ms after 300 ms of speech settling the segment's text and dropping its audio, and a segment of 12 s with no pause settling by agreement the words two passes in a row start with minus the last two; the stop asks for one last pass over what is not settled; Ultra is not measured and gives its text when the person stops, so the box says Transcribing…), Parakeet EOU 120M and Nemotron Streaming EN (English only, NVIDIA Open Model License), Nemotron 3.5 Multilingual (English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, German, Chinese and Japanese, with detection of the language, OpenMDW-1.1) and Parakeet Unified 0.6B (English only, NVIDIA Open Model License), the last four giving partial text natively as the audio arrives, at chunks of about a second.
FluidAudio's `Models.md` also lists models that are not here (Parakeet Redux, Phonon, Bark, Cohere Transcribe, SenseVoice, LFM3-Audio, Paraformer, TDT Japanese, Parakeet TDT v2, Nemotron Unified and others); the list grows by an app update.
The downloads are FluidAudio's own (`FluidAudioDownloader`, the only place FluidAudio goes online, on the person's click, with its offline flag held off while any download runs), into `app/cache/models/fluidaudio/<model>/<repository>/`; each model loads from there with its own `loadLocal` or `loadModels(from:)`, which have no downloader, and with FluidAudio's offline mode on, so a damaged folder fails to start with its cause and never downloads again.
Whisper's four models (`Carrier/WhisperKitRecognizer.swift`: Whisper Tiny, Base, Small and the quantized Large v3 Turbo of the `argmaxinc/whisperkit-coreml` repository, MIT, 99 languages with detection of the language) go through WhisperKit's plain transcription and the core's chunking, not WhisperKit's streaming transcriber, and by the rule of `docs/research/dictation-chunked-partials.md` none of them gets partial text by chunks, so the table says When you stop and the box says Transcribing…: a model must be fast (a real-time factor of 0.05 or less for one pass over 10 s) and steady (10% or less of the words shown rewritten far back), and Tiny is fast but not steady (14.8%), Small and Large v3 Turbo are not fast, and Base was not measured; a change of one catalog field to `.chunked` turns the by-chunks layer on for a model.
A Whisper model that gives its text at the end decodes without word timestamps (the words come from its text) and with `chunkingStrategy: .vad`, which cuts a recording longer than 30 s at its pauses and decodes the pieces in parallel; one that shows partial text by chunks would keep the word timestamps, which the chunking needs, and no cutting.
`WhisperKitDownloader` (WhisperKit's `download` and then the model's tokenizer, which the library would otherwise fetch from the Hub at its first load) writes into `app/cache/models/whisper/<model>/models/<repository>/`; the load is given the folder and told not to download, and refuses with its cause a folder whose tokenizer is gone, since the library would fetch another one.
Parakeet TDT v3 and Ultra transcribe one second of silence when they load, inside `prepare`, and discard the result, so the first inference is not paid by the first dictation and the model counts as ready only after it.
Apple's is the only model with no downloader (`RealDownloads`); under `--fixture` the fixture recognizer lists four invented entries more and `FixtureModelDownloader` shows progress, writes a thousandth of each model's size into a temporary folder and fails one entry with no connection.
Reset (`AppPreferences.reset(.dictation)`) puts the page back to on, Apple's, the language of macOS, Dictate in any app on and Cleanup off, with no server, no model and the default prompt.

## Cleanup

The Cleanup group (`Carrier/CleanupGroup.swift`, over `Carrier/CleanupController.swift`) sets up the model that will clean the text of a dictation ([0030](../adr/0030-dictation-cleanup-runs-on-local-model-servers-through-their-api.md)); it sets the model up and tests it, and the message boxes use it (below).
It has the switch (`dictation.cleanup.enabled`, off by default), the Server popup (`dictation.cleanup.server`: LM Studio or Ollama, with a chip that says Running or Not running, checked when the page opens, when the server changes and when the app comes back to the front), the Model popup (`dictation.cleanup.model`: the models that server lists, and the saved one when the server is down), the Prompt box (`dictation.cleanup.prompt`: it starts with a default that removes filler words and fixes the punctuation and keeps the language that was spoken) and Try it (the sample dictation in a box labelled Dictated, a Clean this sample button and, under it, a box labelled Cleaned with the answer and its seconds; before the first run that box is an outlined hint, while the model works it shows a spinner with Cleaning… and two lines that shimmer (still under Reduce Motion), and when it gives none it is the same box in orange, titled Could not clean, with the cause).
The switch is the whole group's: off, the Server, Model, Prompt and Try it rows are not shown, and the server and model are chosen after turning it on; with it on and no server or model chosen the text says so and a dictation is not cleaned (`cleanupCanBeOn` is what a run needs).
Choosing another server clears the model, since a model belongs to its server.
Clean this sample sends a sample dictation (`cleanupSample`) with the prompt to the chosen model and shows the answer and how long it took, or the cause in orange: the server is not running, the model is not on it, the prompt is empty, no server is chosen, or no answer in 30 seconds.
With Dictation off the screen is drawn at 40% like the others.
The client (`CarrierCore/LocalModelClient.swift`) speaks `GET /v1/models` and `POST /v1/chat/completions` to `127.0.0.1` (LM Studio on 1234, Ollama on 11434), the prompt as the system message and the text as the user message, with no tools and no streaming, and contacts nothing else.

In a message box, with Cleanup on, a dictation that ends with words goes on to clean: the box says Cleaning… where it says Transcribing…, the text of the dictation stays in the box as it was heard, and when the model answers that text, and only that text, is replaced by the cleaned one at the same place, leaving the rest of the box and the caret where they were (`DictationSession`, state `.cleaning`, `DictationController.cleanup()` over `CleanupRun`).
Esc, a click on the microphone (which shows a spinner and can be pressed meanwhile), a key typed in the box, Enter, leaving the session or another box starting a dictation skip the cleanup and keep the raw text; so does a text that is no longer as the dictation left it.
When the server is down, the model is missing or it does not answer in 30 seconds, the raw text stays, the cause shows in red under the box like any failure and a line with the cause only is appended to `dictation.log`.
With Cleanup off, or on a server and model not chosen, a dictation in a box ends exactly as before.

In the widget, with Cleanup on, the end of the dictation (the second press, or the release in a Hold) goes on to clean, on the same session and the same `DictationController.cleanup()` as the boxes (`GlobalDictation` gives it to `session.start(cleanup:)`): the widget shows the raw text shimmering (still under Reduce Motion) and, where the time and the keys were, Cleaning… Esc to skip; the Pill shows a sparkle that pulses. When the model answers, the cleaned text is what is pasted (or, when it cannot be pasted, copied and kept), and the history entry has both texts, the cleanup model and its time.
Esc while it cleans does not cancel the dictation: it skips the cleanup and the raw text goes on to be pasted, as if Cleanup were off; a late answer changes nothing.
When the server is down, the model is missing or it does not answer in 30 seconds, the raw text is pasted anyway, the widget shows a warning (the box says Pasted without cleaning and the cause, the Pill a warning icon) for a few seconds, the entry has no cleaned text, and a line with the cause only is appended to `dictation.log`. If the text could only be copied, the kept widget adds It was not cleaned and the cause. A failure of the recognizer is still a failure, with no paste (`GlobalDictation` tells the two by how the session's take ended: stopped for a cleanup that failed, failed for the recognizer).
With Cleanup off, or on a server and model not chosen, the widget's dictation ends exactly as before.
