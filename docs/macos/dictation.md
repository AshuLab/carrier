# Dictation

Dictation in the message boxes and in any app: the recognizers, the microphone, the Dictation page of [Settings](settings.md), the speech models and the [global dictation](#global-dictation).

## Recognizer and microphone

A real build registers Apple's recognizer (`Carrier/AppleRecognizer.swift`, over SpeechAnalyzer: native partial text, nothing to download, the language chosen on the Dictation page, which starts on the first language of macOS that it supports) and the microphone capture (`Carrier/MicrophoneCapture.swift`, which gives the buffers to the recognizer and the level, `CarrierCore/AudioLevel.swift`, about thirty times a second).
The first click asks macOS for the microphone; a denied one draws `mic.slash` and a click opens a popover with Open System Settings; a failure to start shows its cause in red under the box and is appended, cause only, to `<Carrier folder>/app/logs/dictation.log` (`DictationLog.swift`).
SpeechAnalyzer needs only the microphone permission, not `NSSpeechRecognitionUsageDescription`.

## Under `--fixture`

Under `--fixture` the app registers the fixture recognizer instead (`CarrierCore/FixtureRecognizer.swift`) and asks macOS for nothing: the microphone in the message box of the new session and of a conversation answers a click with a canned Spanish phrase, word by word as partial text (the normal colour with a touch of the accent and a dotted underline) and then final (plain), at the caret.
`--fixture-dictation-at-the-end` gives the phrase only after the click that stops it (the box says "Transcribing…") and `--fixture-dictation-fails` fails to start with a cause shown in red under the box; the global shortcut dictates the same canned phrase into the widget.

## The Dictation page

The Dictation page (`Carrier/DictationTab.swift`, which lists its groups, each its own view: `DictationGroup`, `GlobalDictationGroup`, `CleanupGroup`, `SpeechModelsGroup` and `StorageGroup`; `SettingsPage.dictation`, the App group right after Chat) has the switch (`dictation.enabled`, on by default; off takes the microphone out of both boxes at once, asks macOS for nothing and stops a dictation that listens, keeping its text), the Speech model popup (`dictation.model`; an unknown one reads as Apple's), the Language popup (`dictation.language`; `languageChoices` in `CarrierCore/DictationSettings.swift` says what each model offers) and the microphone's state with Open System Settings when it is blocked, then the Global dictation group (`Carrier/GlobalDictationGroup.swift`: Dictate in any app, `dictation.global`, on by default, which is dimmed with Dictation off and turns the shortcut off with it), then Sounds (below), then Cleanup (below), then the speech models table (`Carrier/SpeechModelsTable.swift`; the rows and what each offers are `speechModelRows` in `CarrierCore/SpeechModelsTable.swift`: a recognizer filter, a radio on the downloaded rows that chooses the model, Download with its size, a progress bar with Cancel, the cause with Retry, a trash, and the licence linking to the model's card) and Storage (the size of the files under `<Carrier folder>/app/models`, with Show in Finder).

## Global dictation

The shortcut, ⌥⇧ Space, dictates in any app (`Carrier/GlobalHotKey.swift`, a system hot key, which needs no permission): it opens a small floating widget over the display of the window in front, at the bottom centre, that never takes the focus from the app the person is in and lets every click through (`Carrier/DictationWidget.swift`).
The widget shows the text as it is said, the time and the keys (`⌥⇧ Space to finish · Esc to cancel`), with the same wait captions as the message box (Getting the model ready…, Transcribing…).
It is a toggle: the shortcut starts the dictation, the next press ends it and pastes the text once at the cursor of the app in front, and the widget closes.
Esc, which Carrier holds only while the widget is open, cancels in any state: nothing is pasted and the clipboard is as it was.
A dictation with no words pastes nothing and closes with no trace.
A failure to start (a blocked microphone, no speech model, the capture) or to recognize shows its cause in red in the widget, which stays until Esc or the shortcut, and a line with the cause only is appended to `dictation.log`.
The first dictation asks macOS for the microphone.

The paste (`Carrier/ClipboardPaste.swift`) saves every item of the clipboard with every type, writes the text marked as transient (clipboard managers skip it) and for this Mac only, sends a ⌘V after the keys of the shortcut are up, waits 200 ms and puts the clipboard back as it was; if the person copied something meanwhile their copy stays.
When the dictation ends, `pasteDecision` (`CarrierCore/PasteDecision.swift`) decides from what the system says about the permission and the field with the cursor (`Carrier/FocusedField.swift`: the focused element of the app in front, its role and subrole, never its value): an editable field with Accessibility on is pasted; a secure field (the subrole says so, or macOS has secure input on), no field at all and an unreadable focus are only copied, and without Accessibility it is never a paste.
A text that was only copied stays in the widget, which grows to a taller box and takes clicks (still without taking the focus), with the text complete and scrollable, the reason in orange (No text field has the cursor; The field is secure; Accessibility is off for Carrier) and the words "It is on the clipboard".
Its buttons are Copy (puts the text on the clipboard again and says Copied) and Dismiss, and Open System Settings (opens Privacy & Security › Accessibility) when the reason is the permission.
It stays until Dismiss, Esc, or the shortcut, which closes it and starts a new dictation; Esc is held by Carrier only while a dictation is open, so a widget left open does not hold it.
The first time the person turns on Dictate in any app (`dictation.accessibilityExplained`, which Reset leaves alone) with Accessibility not allowed, the page explains what it is for (to see which field has the cursor and press ⌘V; it never reads the fields) and then asks macOS; if the person says Not now it asks again next time, and it never explains once it has.
The terminals and the web inputs of apps that build their accessibility tree only for assistive technology may read as no field, so a dictation there is copied: whether to paste anyway is for the owner to decide after trying them.
`GlobalDictationController` (`Carrier/GlobalDictationController.swift`) joins the machine of `CarrierCore/GlobalDictation.swift` to the hot key, the widget and the paste, and registers the shortcut only while the Dictation page's Dictate in any app and Dictation switches are both on.
The widget runs on the one `DictationSession` of the message boxes, so a dictation in a box and one in the widget never run together: the shortcut stops the box's.
The shortcut is fixed for now (the recorder and Hold are later slices).
With Carrier in front on a session, the shortcut does not open the widget: it focuses that session's message box and dictates into it as the microphone button does (the partial text and the listening line), and the shortcut again ends it, with the text kept in the box. In Settings, an epic or any other view of Carrier, and in any other app, it opens the widget, which pastes into Carrier's focused field if there is one.
The rule is `dictationFront` and `dictationRoute(for:)` in `CarrierCore/DictationRoute.swift`: Carrier's main window, or the Settings sheet over it, must be the key window, Settings over a session counts as Settings, and an archived session, which has no message box, uses the widget. A dictation that the widget already runs is ended by the shortcut wherever the person is. With the Dictation switch off, or Dictate in any app, the shortcut is not registered, so nothing happens in Carrier either.

## Sounds

The Sounds group (`Carrier/SoundsGroup.swift`) has a Start sound and an End sound, each with a play button, a popup of three of macOS's own sounds (Tink, Pop and Glass; Tink starts and Pop ends) and a switch, and one Volume from 0 to 100 that starts at 60 and serves both (`dictation.sound.start.on`, `dictation.sound.start`, `dictation.sound.end.on`, `dictation.sound.end` and `dictation.sound.volume`, all put back by `reset(.dictation)`; a saved sound that is not on offer reads as the default).
The sounds belong to the global dictation: the message boxes stay silent.
The start sound plays when the widget's dictation begins to listen; the end sound plays when the shortcut stops listening and goes on to clean and paste, whether or not words were heard; Esc plays no end sound, since nothing goes on, and a dictation that never listened (a blocked microphone, a failure to start) plays none.
A switch off silences its sound and volume 0 silences both, but the play button always plays, at the volume set.
Which sound a moment plays is a pure function, `dictationSound(for:_:)` in `CarrierCore/DictationSounds.swift`, fed by the moments the machine reports (`GlobalDictationMoment`: `listening`, `stopped`, `cancelled`); `Carrier/DictationSoundPlayer.swift` plays it through `NSSound`.

## Speech models

`ModelStore` (`CarrierCore/ModelStore.swift`) owns the states of the models behind a downloader port: a model lives in `app/models/<recognizer>/<model>/` and counts as downloaded when a `complete` file is there, written last, so a folder without it, left by a cancel, a failure or an app that quit, is deleted (at once, or at the next launch); a failure says no connection, no space on the disk or a file that did not check; the model in use and Apple's, which the system manages, have no delete; and nothing outside the listed models can be downloaded (`SpeechModelCatalog.problems` is the rules of the list: a unique id, a licence, a size or Apple's system marker, an https card on an allowed host).
A real build lists Apple's model, four of Whisper's (below) and six of FluidAudio's (`Carrier/FluidAudioRecognizer.swift`, on Core ML, each with the licence and the card of its model): Parakeet TDT v3 and Parakeet Ultra (25 languages with detection of the language, CC-BY-4.0; both are batch models that the core's chunking runs, `CarrierCore/ChunkedPartials.swift`, tested in `ChunkedPartialsTests`: TDT v3 shows partial text, since one pass over 10 s of audio runs at 0.015 of real time, under the rule's 0.05, by transcribing the audio of the open segment again every second of audio, a pause of 150 ms after 300 ms of speech settling the segment's text and dropping its audio, and a segment of 12 s with no pause settling by agreement the words two passes in a row start with minus the last two; the stop asks for one last pass over what is not settled; Ultra is not measured and gives its text when the person stops, so the box says Transcribing…), Parakeet EOU 120M and Nemotron Streaming EN (English only, NVIDIA Open Model License), Nemotron 3.5 Multilingual (English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, German, Chinese and Japanese, with detection of the language, OpenMDW-1.1) and Parakeet Unified 0.6B (English only, NVIDIA Open Model License), the last four giving partial text natively as the audio arrives, at chunks of about a second.
FluidAudio's `Models.md` also lists models that are not here (Parakeet Redux, Phonon, Bark, Cohere Transcribe, SenseVoice, LFM3-Audio, Paraformer, TDT Japanese, Parakeet TDT v2, Nemotron Unified and others); the list grows by an app update.
The downloads are FluidAudio's own (`FluidAudioDownloader`, the only place FluidAudio goes online, on the person's click, with its offline flag held off while any download runs), into `app/models/fluidaudio/<model>/<repository>/`; each model loads from there with its own `loadLocal` or `loadModels(from:)`, which have no downloader, and with FluidAudio's offline mode on, so a damaged folder fails to start with its cause and never downloads again.
Whisper's four models (`Carrier/WhisperKitRecognizer.swift`: Whisper Tiny, Base, Small and the quantized Large v3 Turbo of the `argmaxinc/whisperkit-coreml` repository, MIT, 99 languages with detection of the language) go through WhisperKit's plain transcription and the core's chunking, not WhisperKit's streaming transcriber, and by the rule of `docs/research/dictation-chunked-partials.md` none of them gets partial text by chunks, so the table says When you stop and the box says Transcribing…: a model must be fast (a real-time factor of 0.05 or less for one pass over 10 s) and steady (10% or less of the words shown rewritten far back), and Tiny is fast but not steady (14.8%), Small and Large v3 Turbo are not fast, and Base was not measured; a change of one catalog field to `.chunked` turns the by-chunks layer on for a model.
`WhisperKitDownloader` (WhisperKit's `download` and then the model's tokenizer, which the library would otherwise fetch from the Hub at its first load) writes into `app/models/whisper/<model>/models/<repository>/`; the load is given the folder and told not to download, and refuses with its cause a folder whose tokenizer is gone, since the library would fetch another one.
Apple's is the only model with no downloader (`RealDownloads`); under `--fixture` the fixture recognizer lists four invented entries more and `FixtureModelDownloader` shows progress, writes a thousandth of each model's size into a temporary folder and fails one entry with no connection.
With the switch off the rows under it and the table are drawn at 40% and take no clicks (`dictationRowIsLive`; Storage and Reset stay live), and Reset (`AppPreferences.reset(.dictation)`) puts the page back to on, Apple's, the language of macOS, Dictate in any app on and Cleanup off, with no server, no model and the default prompt.

## Cleanup

The Cleanup group (`Carrier/CleanupGroup.swift`, over `Carrier/CleanupController.swift`) sets up the model that will clean the text of a dictation ([0030](../adr/0030-dictation-cleanup-runs-on-local-model-servers-through-their-api.md)); for now it only sets the model up and tests it, and what a dictation pastes does not change.
It has the switch (`dictation.cleanup.enabled`, off by default), the Server popup (`dictation.cleanup.server`: LM Studio or Ollama, with a chip that says Running or Not running, checked when the page opens, when the server changes and when the app comes back to the front), the Model popup (`dictation.cleanup.model`: the models that server lists, and the saved one when the server is down), the Prompt box (`dictation.cleanup.prompt`: it starts with a default that removes filler words and fixes the punctuation and keeps the language that was spoken) and Test with a sample.
The switch takes no click until a server and one of its models are chosen (`cleanupCanBeOn`), and choosing another server clears the model and turns it off, since a model belongs to its server.
Test sends a sample dictation (`cleanupSample`) with the prompt to the chosen model and shows the answer and how long it took, or the cause in orange: the server is not running, the model is not on it, the prompt is empty, no server is chosen, or no answer in 30 seconds.
With Dictation off the group is drawn at 40% like the rows above it.
The client (`CarrierCore/LocalModelClient.swift`) speaks `GET /v1/models` and `POST /v1/chat/completions` to `127.0.0.1` (LM Studio on 1234, Ollama on 11434), the prompt as the system message and the text as the user message, with no tools and no streaming, and contacts nothing else.
